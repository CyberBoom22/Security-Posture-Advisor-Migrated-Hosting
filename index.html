Right-size your home security spending

Most households pay twice for the same protection. Your mobile carrier probably bundles a security app you never activated. Your internet provider may already block malicious sites at the router. Then you buy an antivirus suite, a VPN, and a password manager on top of all of it — often at a promotional first-year price that quietly triples at renewal.

Security Hub is a free, no-account tool that helps you figure out what you actually need. Tell it how many phones and computers are in your home, how many adults and children need a password vault, who your carrier is, and what you can spend per month. It builds a plan that starts with what you already have, fills only the real gaps, and shows the total cost both at the intro rate and at the renewal rate — which is the number that matters after year one.

The comparison pages break down the four categories people shop for: standalone password managers, all-in-one security suites, VPNs, and antivirus. Each entry lists the intro price, the renewal price, device caps, adult-seat caps, what independent testing says, and the limitations vendors do not put in their ads. Household seat limits are called out explicitly, because several "family" plans host only two adults regardless of how many seats they advertise.

This site is not affiliated with any company it mentions. There are no sponsorships, no affiliate links, and no commissions. Pricing is refreshed from public sources and may lag current promotions, so always confirm on the provider's own page before you buy.

Nothing you enter is stored or transmitted. The advisor runs entirely in your browser — there is no account, no cookie, and no server that sees your answers.
